# Show the ceiling, hide the chain

**Internal research report, 27 Sep 2026.** Scope: UI/UX direction for every Flying Money audience. Sources are the notes in [`research/ui-ux-2026-09-27/`](research/ui-ux-2026-09-27/); repo paths are `path:line` relative to the repo root, taken from `current_app_baseline.md` (commit 26455d9). Not legal advice.

Flying Money should adopt one design idea across every surface: **make the grant (who can spend, how much, where, until when, and what happens to leftovers) the visible, repeated unit of the product, and keep the chain out of sight until someone asks for it.** By September 2026 the industry has settled on that grant shape for agent and family money alike. AP2 mandates, Mastercard agentic tokens, Coinbase spend permissions, MetaMask Advanced Permissions, Ramp agent cards and Greenlight store controls all scope spending by amount, merchant and time, with a human approving once up front and the agent or child spending inside the envelope. Flying Money's certificate is the strictest version of that grant, and its narrowness is a UX asset: the whole promise fits on one line. The current app already has the hard parts. Every flow works end to end, the risk banners are specific, and the holder and till screens are close to consumer grade. Its problems are about orientation and audience fit. Givers are pushed through agent plumbing (an `.env` download, "gas", an unnumbered two-transaction approve). Holders are asked for a PIN three times and never told the money is test money. Cashiers get no unmistakable success moment. Six names describe one payment object and three vocabularies describe one budget's status. The backlog at the end ranks about thirty changes. Most are quick fixes inside `apps/web`. A handful of larger ideas (a permit entry point, rotating payment codes, an in-chat approval path, notifications) touch the normative spec, recorded decisions, or wording that needs legal review, and are flagged as such rather than recommended.

## Every serious product now ships the same grant, and Flying Money's is the easiest to read

The agent-payments field converged on one model during 2025–26: a human creates a scoped, time-boxed grant, and the agent spends inside it without per-transaction prompts. Google's AP2 splits consent into a human-present Cart Mandate and a human-not-present Intent Mandate that gives an agent "authority to execute a transaction within defined constraints" ([AP2](https://ap2-protocol.net/en/)). Mastercard's agentic tokens "can carry task-specific authority and be restricted by agent, merchant, category, spending limit, timeframe or usage rules" ([Mastercard](https://www.mastercard.com/global/en/news-and-trends/stories/2025/agentic-commerce-framework.html)). Coinbase spend permissions carry a token, an allowance per period and start and end times ([coinbase/spend-permissions](https://github.com/coinbase/spend-permissions)). Ramp's agent cards promise that **"the agent can't bypass or raise its own limit"** and make every credential request carry merchant, amount and a free-text rationale ([Ramp](https://agents.ramp.com/cards)). Family money looks the same. Greenlight lets a parent put "$5 only at a local ice-cream shop" and route anything else through a real-time request ([Greenlight Help](https://help.greenlight.com/hc/en-us/articles/235833027-What-restrictions-can-I-put-on-spending)). Monzo lets a parent lock a child's pot "if the money's for a specific purpose" ([Monzo](https://monzo.com/help/monzo-for-under-16s/under-16s-pots)).

Flying Money's certificate maps onto those fields exactly: the seller is a one-item merchant allowlist, the spender key is the agent token, the end date is the expiry, and the locked amount is a cumulative cap. It also has two properties the others lack. There is **no per-period reset**, so a single spent/left/time-left meter is enough, while Coinbase and MetaMask have to explain rolling windows. And **leftovers return to the owner**, a fact no bank-backed allowance needs to state. That second point is worth designing around. About half of US adults hold unspent gift cards ([Budget & Policy brief](https://budgetandpolicy.org/resources-tools/2023/12/Gift-Card-Brief-2023-1.pdf)), and Starbucks' stored-value liability runs to billions ([SEC 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/829224/000082922425000114/sbux-20250928.htm)), so "what's left comes back to you on 12 Oct" is a real reason to trust the product, not fine print.

Human-AI research says where to put the friction. In a 2026 study of an open-source agent, users "granted wide autonomy for advisory and low-stakes tasks but demanded confirmation for irreversible, externally visible actions". They were unhappy when agents acted without a preview, even when the result was rated a success ([arXiv 2607.18257](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18257); a small student-sample preprint). The CHI 2025 plan-then-execute work warns that too much checking "goes against the premise" of delegating at all ([ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3706598.3713218)). For Flying Money the irreversible, externally visible step is **locking the money**, not signing each slip. The confirmation weight belongs on the issue review. After that, slips inside the envelope need visibility (a meter and a feed), not prompts.

The same research requires honesty about revocation. Coinbase and Ramp can offer a kill switch because a permission manager or card issuer can refuse future spends ([coinbase/spend-permissions](https://github.com/coinbase/spend-permissions); [Ramp blog](https://ramp.com/blog/virtual-cards-for-ai-agents)). Flying Money's contract has no admin and no early cancel. The UI must say so **at the moment of commitment**, where the wizard already says "No early cancel" (`apps/web/components/app/issue-wizard.tsx:660-707`). It must never show a control that implies instant clawback. What a user would most regret not knowing is exactly this: the spender can use the money until the end date, and the owner cannot stop it. The designed answers are small amounts, short end dates and a one-tap Renew.

The practical outcome is one shared component, a **grant summary**, used everywhere a budget appears: "Up to 20.00 USDC · at Lantern Café · spent by Mia's phone · until Fri 24 Oct · what's left returns to you then · can't be cancelled early". Today the pieces of that sentence are spread across the wizard review (`issue-wizard.tsx:709-744`), the request review facts (`apps/web/app/app/requests/new/request-review.tsx:233-366`), the account row (`apps/web/components/account/budget-row.tsx:77-80`), the wallet card (`apps/web/app/wallet/wallet-client.tsx:277-357`) and the certificate page Terms (`apps/web/app/c/[chain]/[id]/page.tsx:50-186`), each worded differently. The copy deck's model sentence, "Give Mia 20.00 USDC at Lantern Café for 30 days. Unused money returns to you on 24 Oct.", is already the right template.

## Owners meet crypto plumbing before they meet the budget

The owner and funder side is where non-crypto users drop out, and the evidence on why is consistent. The consumer default in 2026 is a wallet created for you at sign-in, with gas sponsored and connect-wallet kept as a secondary option. Coinbase Smart Wallet creates a self-custodial wallet in one passkey prompt with no install and no recovery phrase ([Coinbase Help](https://help.coinbase.com/en/wallet/getting-started/smart-wallet)). Privy creates wallets automatically on email, social or passkey sign-up ([Privy](https://www.privy.io/wallets)). Independent conversion data is scarce, and most published figures are vendor claims, so the case rests on step counts rather than measured lifts.

Flying Money's owner app accepts only an injected browser wallet (`apps/web/lib/wagmi.ts:21`, decision D18 at `docs/DECISIONS.md:26`). On a phone without an extension that is a dead end: "No browser wallet found. Install MetaMask…" (`apps/web/components/account/shell.tsx:136-138`). A parent who wants to give a canteen budget needs a desktop wallet holding both USDC and ETH on Arbitrum Sepolia, and the account area never says how to get either. Faucets appear only on `/chains/[chain]` (`apps/web/app/chains/[chain]/page.tsx:90-100`), and a zero balance on Home gives no next step (`apps/web/components/account/home.tsx:72-77`), the "$0.00 with no guidance" dead end that wallet teardowns criticise ([Masterly](https://www.themasterly.com/blog/crypto-wallet-ux-teardown)).

The biggest single defect is that **the person-gift path runs through the agent wizard**. `/app/give?for=person` presets a generated key with no holder name, so the giver is made to "Download the agent .env" and tick "I saved the key" before they can create anything. Afterwards they are told to "Add it to your agent's config" (`apps/web/components/account/give.tsx:41`, `issue-wizard.tsx:218-219`, `:631-652`, `:375-395`). Step 2 still recommends "Bring an agent address" in person mode (`issue-wizard.tsx:566`). The holder-page path already does the right thing: with `holderName` set it skips the download and ends on "send the hand-over link below privately" (`issue-wizard.tsx:375-378`). That branch should become the Home default. Renew has a related bug: it passes the place name as the holder, so the success screen tells the giver to hand the budget to the café (`apps/web/app/app/people/[id]/holder-client.tsx:231-243`).

Funding takes two wallet transactions, and only the first is numbered ("1 · Approve", `issue-wizard.tsx:745-765`, and the same in top-up, `apps/web/components/app/funder-actions.tsx:198`). Of all the words in the funding flow, "approve" carries the most risk. Industry analyses attribute most wallet-drainer losses to users signing approvals they did not understand ([Trust Wallet](https://trustwallet.com/blog/security/token-approvals-and-wallet-drainers-how-to-keep-your-assets-safe); [Group-IB](https://www.group-ib.com/resources/knowledge-hub/crypto-wallet-drainers/)). A January 2026 experiment (N=128) found that turning signing requests into plain-language, labelled summaries improved how accurately users spotted risky signatures, raised their confidence and lowered their workload ([arXiv 2601.16751](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16751)). The app already approves the exact amount and never asks twice (commit 19ce948). What is missing is the words: "Step 1 of 2: let the Flying Money contract move exactly 20.00 USDC from your wallet. Nothing is sent yet." followed by "Step 2 of 2: lock 20.00 USDC for Lantern Café until Fri 24 Oct."

There are three cleaner technical paths, and none should be adopted without a decision. USDC implements ERC-2612 permit, so a contract entry point that accepted a permit signature would turn approve plus issue into one signature and one transaction ([EIP-2612](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-2612)). That changes the contract (BUILD_SPEC §7) and its audit surface, so it is **flagged, not recommended**. EIP-7702, live since the May 2025 Pectra upgrade, lets an existing EOA batch approve and fund, and have gas sponsored, without changing its address ([Turnkey](https://www.turnkey.com/blog/account-abstraction-erc-4337-eip-7702)). That needs no contract change and fits the ECDSA-only spender rule, because the spender key stays an EOA. It depends on wallet support, and sponsorship implies a hosted paymaster, which the marketing report flags as closer to "operating a service" than publishing software. WalletConnect or an embedded wallet would unblock phones but reverses D18. All three belong in the flagged table below.

Transaction feedback is sound in the happy path. States run Preparing → Confirm in your wallet → Submitted → Confirming → Confirmed/Failed (`apps/web/components/app/tx.tsx:17-25`), and wallet errors are mapped to plain words (`apps/web/lib/tx-errors.ts:29-46`). Two gaps remain. Success is **implicit** for top up, extend and take back: the panel closes and the list refetches, so the "Confirmed" line vanishes (`funder-actions.tsx:74-79`). And the copy still uses "gas" ("pays no gas", `issue-wizard.tsx:561`; "doesn't have enough ETH for gas", `tx-errors.ts:36`), against the copy deck's own rule (`docs/copy/copy-deck.md:205`). The standard mapping holds: EIP-1193 code 4001 means the user cancelled and deserves neutral copy ("You cancelled in your wallet. Nothing was sent."), and 4902 means the chain must be added before switching ([MetaMask](https://docs.metamask.io/metamask-connect/troubleshooting/)). Research on wallet users shows persistent beliefs that transactions are reversible and cancellable ([CHI 2021](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3411764.3445407)). That is one more reason every lock confirmation should end with its irreversibility stated plainly.

On phones the account area also hides its "+ Give a budget" button and its only test-money notice below the `lg` breakpoint (`shell.tsx:60`, `:72-97`). The six-item nav becomes an unmarked horizontal scroll (`shell.tsx:33-34`). Unbounce's 57M-conversion dataset found **79% of landing-page visits came from mobile** ([Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/saas-conversion-rate/)). Even a devtool cannot assume a desktop owner.

## Agents should ask like a child asks a parent, and owners should answer like a manager

Flying Money's request inbox (`apps/web/components/account/requests.tsx:31-174`, review at `request-review.tsx:233-366`) is already closer to best practice than most shipped products. It shows who asks with a signature check, sets the agent's rationale apart as "Written by the agent, not verified", tiers risk banners for unknown agents and unknown services, and requires a vouch checkbox when both are unknown (`request-review.tsx:139-160`, D37). The external reference points are Apple's Ask to Buy, which shows who is asking, what the item is and what it costs, with Approve and Decline and a notice back to the child on decline ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/105055)); Ramp's required rationale field ([Ramp](https://agents.ramp.com/cards)); and MetaMask's permission requests, where "the user can modify the permission parameters" before approving ([MetaMask docs](https://docs.metamask.io/smart-accounts-kit/concepts/advanced-permissions/)).

Measured against them, the review screen needs four refinements. It already allows lowering the amount and forbids shortening below the requested days (`issue-wizard.tsx:338-365`). **Counter-offer should be a named action** ("Approve a smaller budget"), and Decline should get equal visual weight. Top-up requests can't be approved at all ("can't be approved from this page yet", `request-review.tsx:121-133`), which breaks the loop for the most common follow-up ask. Anti-fatigue controls are absent: nothing shows "this agent asked 3 times today", and nothing collapses duplicates. And the agent-side vocabulary still sends owners to "the Counting House" (`packages/mcp/src/server.ts:104`), a name the owner app no longer uses.

Inside chat, the constraint is sharper. The MCP elicitation spec (2025-11-25) says servers "MUST NOT use form mode elicitation to request … payment credentials" and must use URL mode for payments. Clients must show the full URL and get consent before opening it, and servers must bind the elicitation to the same user so an attacker cannot forward the link to a victim ([MCP spec](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-11-25/client/elicitation)). OpenAI's Apps SDK caps inline cards at "up to two actions maximum" ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/apps-sdk/concepts/ui-guidelines)). Richer in-chat approval UIs are still an open proposal ([ext-apps #511](https://github.com/modelcontextprotocol/ext-apps/issues/511)). The right design is the one the repo already half-has. The agent's `fm_request_budget` returns a link or posts to the inbox (`server.ts:280-330`), and the owner signs in the web app, never in chat. The upgrades are to emit that link as a URL-mode elicitation where clients support it, falling back to plain text; to return a compact post-payment line ("paid 0.02 · 0.37 of 0.50 used · ends 12 Oct"); and to report decline and "not answered yet" as different outcomes, so the agent doesn't re-ask straight after a no. Changing MCP tool behaviour touches the SDK surface (§8), so this is flagged. The marketing report's constraint also applies: Anthropic's directory policy bars software that executes financial transactions on users' behalf, so in-chat UX should assume the open MCP registry, not a first-party connector.

For agent owners, the budget detail view should become a **meter plus a feed**. The honest meter can only show what the owner can know. Slips are off-chain between agent and seller, so the owner sees on-chain collections, not unredeemed slips. The meter should therefore read "Collected by the seller: 0.21 · Could still be collected: up to 0.29 · Ends in 3 days · What's left returns to you on 12 Oct", not a "spent" figure the owner cannot verify. Where the owner also runs the agent (the demo, the SDK), the agent's local running total can feed a per-slip list, following the demo's "what each slip adds" labelling (commit 74ffa1f).

## Holders need a gift card's calm, and cashiers need a soundbox's certainty

The holder wallet is the closest thing to a consumer product in the repo. It has a narrow single column, a numeric PIN keypad, a rear-camera scanner, a full-screen light QR with wake lock, and an install nudge (`wallet-client.tsx:262-270`, `:590-608`, `:359-384`). Three things let it down. First-time receipt asks for the PIN **three times**: set, repeat, then again to add (`wallet-client.tsx:233-234`, `:711`). The wallet is the one surface with **no test-money or unaudited notice at all** (`apps/web/app/wallet/page.tsx:14-23`), although its users arrive from a link and see "USDC left". They are the people most likely to take test money for real. And every card ends in a raw "Only valid at 0x…" (`wallet-client.tsx:340-346`), even though the giver named the place during hand-over.

Consumer patterns give a clear target. Starbucks puts the balance first, the pay code one tap away and history below ([Starbucks](https://www.starbucks.com/rewards/mobile-apps/)). Venmo's gift-wrap turns a transfer into a short "unwrap" moment ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/01/13/venmo-introduces-new-gift-wrapping-feature-with-eight-animated-designs/)). Monzo lets kids name pots and add a picture ([Monzo](https://monzo.com/help/monzo-for-under-16s/under-16s-pots)). For Flying Money that means a skippable reveal on first open that ends on the facts ("20.00 test USDC for Lantern Café, from Mum, until Fri 24 Oct. What's left goes back to Mum."), then "Add to home screen" as the first action so the link isn't lost in a chat thread. After that comes one PIN set-and-confirm with no third entry. The card itself should show the place name, an optional emoji, the amount left in large numerals, the end date with a month name, and the leftover line.

Backup deserves specific care. Peer-reviewed work on seed phrases (CHI 2025, N=643) reports that many users think a seed phrase can be reset like a password ([ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3706598.3713209); percentages relayed via secondary summaries). The wallet's passphrase-sealed file (`wallet-client.tsx:386-461`) has the same risk profile. It should be named for what it is ("A backup file locked with a passphrase you choose. Nobody can reset it."), and the app should decrypt the downloaded file once as a test before calling the backup done. The restore order also needs fixing: a new phone is made to set a new PIN, and the restore then silently replaces it with the backup's PIN (`wallet-client.tsx:103-109`, `:442-445`; `apps/web/lib/wallet.ts:199-202`). Flying Money can also make a trust statement a general wallet can't: "If you lose this phone, the unspent money goes back to Mum on 24 Oct."

The till has to meet the standard India set at the counter. Merchants there faced fake payment screenshots, and Paytm's answer was a device that **speaks the received amount aloud from the merchant's own verified source**. Its merchant guidance says "Don't Rely Only on Screenshots" ([Paytm](https://paytm.com/support/paytm-soundbox/how-merchants-can-verify-payment-receipts); [Paytm Soundbox](https://business.paytm.com/soundbox)). Flying Money is structurally right for this. The holder shows a customer-presented code, Alipay-style ([Antom](https://global.alipay.com/product-detail.htm?bizCode=OPEN_OFFLINE_PAYMENT-Barcode)), and the till verifies it locally, so the truth lives on the cashier's device. But the ResultCard (`apps/web/app/shop/[chain]/[payee]/pos/pos-client.tsx:269-322`) is a panel with a small seal, not a moment. It should fill the screen with the amount in the largest type on the page, play a chime with an optional spoken amount in the shop's language, use a clearly different sound and colour for failure, and announce the result in an ARIA live region. Failure copy should lead with impact and avoid blaming the customer. Style guidance from Monzo and Mailchimp favours specific, calm errors with a next step ([Monzo](https://monzo.com/tone-of-voice); [Mailchimp](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/)). So "Rejected" becomes "Not accepted: this budget has ended. Ask the customer for another way to pay", and "Unverified · merchant risk" becomes "Checked on this device only. You could lose 5.00 if it's bad."

Offline is Flying Money's edge, and regulated rails are converging on the same design. UPI Lite X works over NFC without internet at low caps, and Brazil's central bank is building an offline Pix that records on-device and syncs later ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/news/upi-lite-will-let-users-send-and-receive-money-offline-everything-you-need-to-know); [meutudo](https://meutudo.com.br/blog/noticias/2026/08/14/pix-sem-internet-relatorio-do-bc-revela-atualizacoes-incluindo-imposto-por-transacao/)). Staff copy should borrow that framing: "Checked on this device. The money arrives when you collect." It should show a queue count and a deadline ("3 payments waiting · collect before Fri"). Collect is currently disabled with no reason when the wallet is disconnected, on the wrong network or out of gas; only offline gets a message (`pos-client.tsx:414-424`, also audit F-series). The copy deck's "Collected. [n] payments in one transfer." was never built (`pos-client.tsx:383-386`).

Accessibility at the counter matters more than elsewhere. Section508.gov says a QR code should never be the only path, and the wallet's "Copy the code instead" partly satisfies that ([Section508.gov](https://www.section508.gov/blog/accessibility-bytes/qr-codes/)). Blind users find QR-based UPI slow and impractical ([Semantic Scholar](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/4377/c094eb30dd889fc2cb1ad8fdea1a476d91e6.pdf)). A human-readable short code that could be read aloud or typed would help, and so would a rotating element to blunt screenshot replay. Both change the slip format (BUILD_SPEC §6), so both are flagged. The full-screen pay overlay is a plain `<section>` with no focus handling or Escape (`wallet-client.tsx:604-608`), and the till keypad is unreachable by keyboard (`pos-client.tsx:194-206`). Both are fixable in `apps/web`. Delegated use is common in low-literacy settings, where relatives and shopkeepers transact for others, often with shared PINs ([ACM COMPASS 2026](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3811242.3819111)). A budget scoped to one shop and one date is a safer substitute for that, and the helper persona deserves copy of its own.

## Developers and first-time visitors judge the whole project by its first minute

For developers the metric is time to first success. Postman found that ready-to-run collections cut time-to-first-call 1.7x at one company and up to 56x at others ([Postman](https://blog.postman.com/improve-your-time-to-first-api-call-by-20x/)). The marketing report sets the matching activation event: **the first slip verified by a seller, in under five minutes on testnet**. The docs patterns that serve this are well established. Stripe pre-fills test keys in samples and gives every error a `doc_url` pointing at its code ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/api/errors)). Mintlify, Hugging Face and PostHog serve every page as Markdown with "Copy page / Open in Claude / Open in ChatGPT" menus ([Mintlify](https://github.com/mintlify/docs/pull/727/files); [HF](https://huggingface.co/changelog/docs-llms-txt)). Checkly found that Claude Code, Cursor and OpenCode send `Accept: text/markdown`, and that a docs page shrank from about 180k tokens as HTML to 478 as Markdown ([Checkly](https://www.checklyhq.com/blog/state-of-ai-agent-content-negotation/)). Flying Money already serves `/llms.txt`, `/llms-full.txt` and a Markdown twin per docs page (`apps/web/app/docs/[slug]/page.tsx:18-34`). What's missing is content negotiation on the HTML URL, the page menu, a sidebar grouped by Diátaxis type (tutorials: agents, client, server; reference: protocol, contract; explanation: guarantees, story) ([Diátaxis](https://diataxis.fr/)), and a glossary. On phones the full ten-link DocsNav renders above every page (`apps/web/components/docs-nav.tsx:3-32`). Samples should pull chain, contract and USDC addresses from `@flying-money/chains`, never hard-code them. Stable SDK error codes with doc links would copy Stripe, but they change the SDK surface (§8) and are flagged.

The `/demo` belongs to the same genre as Cloudflare's x402 playground, which creates and funds a testnet wallet on open and lets payments run with or without human confirmation ([Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/x402)). Flying Money's difference is the ceiling, so the demo should make the ceiling the climax. It should show a meter falling with each slip and a plainly labelled "refused: over the budget" moment, and it should separate off-chain steps (a slip signed and checked in milliseconds) from on-chain ones (the batch collection, with an explorer link). The "Steal the agent key" toggle (`apps/web/app/demo/demo-client.tsx:183-274`) already tells the bounded-loss story. It should end on the sentence the marketing report centres on: the most it can ever cost is what you put in.

The landing page has one structural issue and several small ones. It uses a two-door hero keyed to "Who is spending?" (`apps/web/components/door.tsx:9-87`). NN/g warns against audience-based navigation as the primary route: users don't know which group they belong to, and they worry that another group gets a better deal. It is acceptable as secondary navigation with "For X" labels ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/audience-based-navigation/)). The toggle is a fair compromise because it swaps only the example, not the product. But the people door routes to the café explainer and the till, never to giving (`apps/web/components/hero.tsx:51-65`). `/shops` sends givers to the wallet-gated `/app` rather than `/app/give` (`apps/web/app/shops/page.tsx:69-74`). The wallet is reachable on desktop only from the footer (`apps/web/components/site-footer.tsx:36-45`). The positioning line "Hand over a budget, not your wallet." is a screen-reader-only `<h1>` that sighted visitors never see (`hero.tsx:14-32`). Evil Martians' review of 100+ devtool pages favours a specific headline, one visual and two CTAs, with problem-first storytelling ([Evil Martians](https://evilmartians.com/chronicles/we-studied-100-devtool-landing-pages-here-is-what-actually-works-in-2025)). Unbounce found that copy at a 5th–7th grade reading level converted at **12.9% against 2.1%** for professional-register copy ([Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/saas-conversion-rate/)). Below the hero, a three-card "For agent builders / For API sellers / For families and shops" band, each card with one CTA, gives sellers a route they lack today. The seller quickstart currently surfaces only in chapter 5 (`apps/web/app/page.tsx:282-299`).

Trust signals follow credibility research more than fashion. In the Stanford Web Credibility Project, "design look" was the factor people cited most, and the guidelines stress making claims easy to verify and avoiding errors of every kind ([Stanford](https://credibility.stanford.edu/guidelines/index.html)). The aesthetic-usability effect forgives minor issues but not broken ones ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/aesthetic-usability-effect/)). The paper, ink and seal system (`apps/web/app/globals.css:3-208`) is a real asset against the 2026 "AI slop" look of Inter, purple gradients and three rounded cards ([925studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-design-tells)), and it should be kept. Four rules keep it honest. Put the metaphor in artefacts (certificate card, seal, tally), not in controls, following NN/g's point that skeuomorphism helps learning but hurts when it spreads into interactions ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/skeuomorphism/)). Show the seal only when something was signed or verified, as the spec already says (`docs/BUILD_SPEC.md:1410`). Give errors a hue other than seal red, because red means authenticity on a Chinese seal and failure in a Western finance UI. And caption 飛錢 with its romanisation and meaning, with the history checked, because characters used as decoration read as a "flattened, smooth and harmless Other" ([US-China Today](https://uschinatoday.org/qa/2019/12/19/when-cultural-appreciation-becomes-appropriation-lei-ping-on-chinese-imagery-in-western-fashion/)). The `/guarantees` page should follow Aave's risks page, listing each risk next to its mitigation ([Aave](https://aave.com/docs/resources/risks)): each invariant as a promise, how it is enforced, how it is tested, and what it does not cover, with a `SECURITY.md` disclosure route.

The accessibility baseline is good: a skip link, labelled forms, `aria-live` transaction status, and motion gated by `prefers-reduced-motion`. It still has known WCAG misses. The European Accessibility Act has applied since 28 June 2025. Sources disagree on whether it maps to WCAG 2.1 or 2.2, so targeting 2.2 AA covers both readings ([Level Access](https://www.levelaccess.com/compliance-overview/european-accessibility-act-eaa/)). The misses are: celadon (#7fa38f) used as text on paper despite the token's "fills, not text" note (`budget-row.tsx:20`, `request-review.tsx:251`); a StepExplorer that auto-advances every 4.2 s, ignores reduced motion and announces each change (`apps/web/components/step-explorer.tsx:30-34`, `:82-84`); tablists with no tabpanels or arrow keys (`apps/web/components/account/budgets.tsx:27-40`, `pos-client.tsx:87-106`); and a Testnet badge whose explanation lives in a hover-only `title` (`apps/web/components/site-header.tsx:37-42`). There are no route-level `error.tsx` or `loading.tsx` files anywhere under `apps/web/app`.

## Words decide both comprehension and legal exposure

One payment object has four names: payment slip, payment code, code and note (`certificate-lists.tsx:230`, `pos-client.tsx:240`, `wallet-client.tsx:315`, `issue-wizard.tsx:561`). The Collect page's button says "Redeem" (`certificate-lists.tsx:248`). A budget's status reads Active / Ending soon / Ended in account rows but Open / Expired / Closed on the certificate page, and "ending" means 2 days in one place and 3 in another (`budget-row.tsx:15-24`, `holder-client.tsx:204`). The owner area is "Account" in the nav, "Counting House" in the wallet and MCP, and "Dashboard" on the certificate page (`site-header.tsx:78`, `wallet-client.tsx:799`, `apps/web/app/c/[chain]/[id]/page.tsx:117-118`). BUILD_SPEC §21.3, DECISIONS D31 and the code each name different tabs. SOUPS 2020 found that a tool's terms teach users their mental model ([USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/soups2020-mai.pdf)), so this drift has consequences beyond cosmetics. The fix is a single glossary constant that the UI imports, a denylist test in `pnpm verify` for retired words, and a public `/docs/glossary` with three columns (code term, agent surface, people surface), following Stripe's single glossary ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/glossary)). AI agents especially need the synonym mapping stated, or they will treat "budget" and "certificate" as different objects.

Choosing the people-facing noun is not purely a UX decision. The copy deck wants "certificate" on people screens and "budget" on agent screens (`docs/copy/copy-deck.md:63-64`), while the code uses "budget" everywhere. "Certificate" brings the right model (prepaid money for one shop), but it also brings two legal collisions. US Regulation E §1005.20 bars selling gift certificates whose funds expire less than five years after issue ([CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1005/20/)), and a "certificate" with "money set aside until an end date" resembles a certificate of deposit. FDIC Part 328, fully in force since 1 Jan 2025, puts crypto-asset representations in scope ([eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-328/subpart-B)). Whether either rule reaches a non-custodial contract where nothing is forfeited is a question for counsel. The UX recommendation is narrower: keep "budget" in the UI until counsel rules; never pair "certificate" with "gift", "deposit", "interest" or "term"; and always state that leftovers return to the giver, which is the opposite of the harm the gift-card rules address.

The same review should cover wording the app already ships. The nav label "Account" and the copy deck's till hint "to your account" invite the bank model that SOUPS calls "bank bias". The till's **"Guaranteed: backed by money set aside for your shop"** (`pos-client.tsx:18-32`) and the `/guarantees` route clash with the marketing report's banned-word list, which includes "guaranteed". That wording comes from the spec's own vocabulary ("Accepted by seller (GUARANTEED)", `docs/BUILD_SPEC.md:1446-1461`), so changing it is a spec-and-legal call, not a copy tweak. Amounts should be formatted from `bigint` with locale-aware separators, keeping "USDC" as a unit label, because USDC is not an ISO currency and `Intl` currency style won't know it. Dates should always use a month name. Input must parse "3,50" correctly, and the issue wizard currently rejects it (`issue-wizard.tsx:186`) while top-up and the till accept it ([W3C i18n](https://w3c.github.io/i18n-drafts/questions/qa-number-format.en.html)).

## Conclusion

The research shifts the design question from "how do we make crypto approachable" to "how do we make a promise legible". Incumbents put caps on agent and family spending as settings someone else can change. Flying Money's cap is money already set aside, so its UI can say something theirs cannot: the worst case, in one sentence, before anyone signs. Almost every recommendation above serves that sentence. It should be shown at commit time, repeated on every card, performed by the demo, and kept free of words that promise more than the contract does. The largest gains are cheap: about fifteen quick fixes remove most of the friction a non-crypto giver, holder or cashier would hit today, and none of them touches the contract.

The less obvious implication is that the hard constraints are design material, not obstacles. No early cancel becomes a clear pre-commit disclosure and a Renew habit. Local verification becomes the till's soundbox moment. The one-seller rule becomes a grant summary short enough to read at a glance. The open questions are empirical and legal, not visual: which noun people understand (card sort and comprehension tests), whether the words certificate, account and guaranteed survive counsel, and whether a phone-first owner path is worth reopening D18. Those should be answered before the large redesigns, and before any consumer surface carries real money.
