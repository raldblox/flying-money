# Flying Money: marketing research (27 Sep 2026)

**Internal. Not for the public docs site (`docs/site/`).** Go-to-market research across developers, sellers, people and AI agents. Sourced notes behind it are in [`research/marketing-2026-09-27/`](research/marketing-2026-09-27/). Not legal advice.

## Sell the ceiling, not the rail

Flying Money should be marketed as the simplest, most honest way to put a hard ceiling on what an agent or a person can spend: a prepaid budget for one seller, with an end date, that no one can raise. It should not be marketed as a new payment rail. The evidence points one way. Consumers' top fear about agent payments is overspending: **60% worry an AI could spend beyond intended amounts** ([NMI via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/nmi-research-consumers-want-ai-130000894.html)), and **spending caps (30%) and instant revocation (29%)** top their non-negotiables ([Checkout.com](https://www.checkout.com/newsroom/consumer-demand-for-ai-shopping-is-forming-fast-but-trust-for-agentic-commerce-is-still-catching-up)). Meanwhile the rail layer is crowded and well funded, and Stripe and Tempo's MPP sessions already ship the same escrow-plus-cumulative-voucher mechanism on mainnet ([Tempo](https://tempo.xyz/blog/mpp-sessions/)). So Flying Money cannot win on "novel mechanism" or on distribution. It can win on a claim nobody else makes cleanly: *bounded loss by construction*. The worst case is the locked amount, it can go only to the named seller, it is capped in immutable code at 100 USDC, and there is no admin key, fee or token. For a small team with no budget, that message has to travel through developer channels, integrations and earned third-party mentions, not paid ads. Paid crypto ads mostly need licences Flying Money does not have ([Google Ads policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/14009787?hl=en)), and the project is still testnet and unaudited, which rules out any consumer push for real money today. The plan at the end sequences the work: developers and sellers first, agents' discovery surfaces second, and consumers only after an audit and a human-approved mainnet deploy.

## Buyers want a ceiling; incumbents sell a policy

Every piece of demand research in the notes converges on one anxiety, and it is not "how do agents pay". Only **23% of US adults trust generative AI to handle payments on its own**, even though 72% have used an AI assistant ([Visa Trust Index](https://investor.visa.com/news/news-details/2026/New-Visa-Research-Finds-Consumer-Trust-is-Accelerating-the-Path-to-Agentic-Commerce/default.aspx)). In NMI's August 2026 survey, **70% need to review or override before purchase**, 76% are uncomfortable with AI completing purchases alone, and 84% have never uploaded payment details to an AI tool ([NMI via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/nmi-research-consumers-want-ai-130000894.html)). Consumers will still delegate small, routine purchases: on average they would let an agent spend about **£177 per purchase** without approval, and 41% would delegate groceries ([Checkout.com](https://www.checkout.com/newsroom/consumer-demand-for-ai-shopping-is-forming-fast-but-trust-for-agentic-commerce-is-still-catching-up)). The 100 USDC per-certificate cap sits in that comfort zone. Marketing should present it as the right size by design, not as a limitation.

The incumbents have heard the same thing and are answering with **policy**. Visa's OpenAI integration offers "user-defined guardrails (spend cap, merchant categories, human approval)" ([Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2026/06/12/visa-openai-agent-led-payments/)). Mastercard's Agentic Tokens bind a card to an agent, a merchant scope and a consent policy ([Eco, secondary](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/15192001-what-is-mastercard-agent-pay-ai-agent-commerce-protocol-in-2026)). Coinbase Spend Permissions let a spender draw, for example, 10 USDC a month from a live smart account ([Coinbase docs](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/server-wallets/v2/evm-features/spend-permissions)). "Cap", "scope" and "budget" are becoming table stakes, so Flying Money cannot own the words. It can own the mechanism behind them. A platform guardrail is a setting that someone (the issuer, the platform, a bug, a compromised account) can change. An allowance draws on a wallet that still holds everything else. A Flying Money budget is money already set aside that can only reach one seller before one date, and whatever is left goes back to the owner. The tagline "Hand over a budget, not your wallet" already says this. The supporting line should be concrete: *the most it can ever cost you is what you put in*.

Honest demand data helps here. x402 is the category's loudest proof point, and its own numbers show that the handshake is not the bottleneck. In March 2026 real volume was about **$28,000 a day at an average of $0.20**, with roughly half of activity classed as gamed ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/03/11/coinbase-backed-ai-payments-protocol-wants-to-fix-micropayment-but-demand-is-just-not-there-yet)). Chainalysis found a meme-coin episode driving a Q4 2025 surge, with weekly retention peaking at 87% and then falling to 5%. It also found payments of **$1 or more rising from 49% to 95% of volume** ([Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/x402-agentic-payments-adoption/)). Two conclusions follow. First, agent payments are moving toward dollar-sized purchases, which suits prepaid bundles better than sub-cent streaming. Second, inflated metrics are a known sore spot, so a project that reports only independent, paid redemptions will stand out.

## Position as the neutral budget layer, not a rival rail

The competitive map in September 2026 has two camps. Card-network and checkout rails (Visa Intelligent Commerce and TAP, Mastercard Agent Pay, ACP, Google AP2) frame the problem as trust, consent and identity. Stablecoin machine-payment rails (x402, Tempo MPP, Circle Nanopayments, L402) frame it as cheap per-request settlement. Venture money has gone to custodial or platform-mediated "agent banks": **Natural raised a $30M Series A** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/natural-raises-30m-to-reinvent-payments-for-ai-agents-and-take-on-stripe/)) and **Sapiom has raised $50M in total** ([Sapiom](https://www.sapiom.ai/resources/blog/series-a/)). None of the funded players markets a non-custodial, intermediary-free, single-seller prepaid budget as its core product. That gap is Flying Money's position. It is narrow, and it is real.

|---|---|---|---|

No chargebacks for sellers. Open to anyone with no application. |

The overall stance should be **complementary and candid**. Flying Money limits *what* a key can spend no matter *who* the agent is. That complements the identity-first "know your agent" narrative that a16z and Skyfire promote ([a16z crypto](https://a16zcrypto.com/posts/article/big-ideas-things-excited-about-crypto-2026/)) rather than competing with it. The honest weaknesses should appear on the comparison page itself, because the audiences that matter reward candour. The weaknesses are these. Each budget pays one seller, so an agent calling ten APIs needs ten budgets and its capital is split. The owner must hold USDC on Arbitrum. The project is testnet and unaudited. Spender keys must be ECDSA, which excludes the smart-account and passkey spenders that Coinbase and Circle wallets use. And there is no ecosystem directory yet. Stating these up front costs little: the people who hit them would find them within an hour anyway, and saying so first earns credibility with the rest.

## Four audiences need four different doors

### Developers and agent builders: MCP first, then adapters, then launches

For agent infrastructure, the proven path is to show up where builders already work. Stripe's agent toolkit shipped as one library with adapters for LangChain, CrewAI and the Vercel AI SDK, later adding the OpenAI Agents SDK and MCP ([Stripe toolkit](https://github.com/DTTconnect/stripe-agent-toolkit)). An independent July 2026 analysis of E2B advises smaller players to **"ship the MCP server first"**, take cooperative framework slots selectively, and skip unilateral templates because they are costly to maintain ([bex.co](https://bex.co/blog/2026/07/08/e2b-openai-agents-sdk-sandbox-neutrality)). Flying Money already has an MCP server and TypeScript SDKs, so the zero-budget move is packaging, not new building. That means thin tool adapters for the Vercel AI SDK and Mastra (TypeScript-native), then the OpenAI Agents SDK and LangChain.js, each under 20 lines and each making a real capped payment against the testnet demo seller. On the seller side, the Hono middleware should gain Next.js route-handler and Cloudflare Workers examples, which mirror where x402 got embedded. Composio shows the durable SEO form of this: one page per tool × framework pair ([Composio](https://composio.dev/toolkits/browserbase_tool/framework/open-ai-agents-sdk)).

Launches create spikes, not durable reach, and the team should plan with that in mind. An analysis of 188,085 Show HN posts puts the **median score at 2**, with only 6% reaching 50 or more. Stars convert at roughly **1.4 per upvote**, and 92% of the star gain is over within 48 hours. The best slot is Monday 00:00 UTC, which is Sunday evening US Eastern ([Daniel King](https://danfking.github.io/blog/2026/04/23/show-hn-by-the-numbers/)). HN rewards a plain one-sentence title, an open-source flag, a founder first comment that explains the problem and the trade-offs, and deep replies to every comment ([Markepear](https://www.markepear.dev/blog/dev-tool-hacker-news-launch)). Supabase's pattern of a five-day launch week, one feature a day with the engineer who built it writing the post ([Supabase](https://supabase.com/blog/supabase-how-we-launch)), turns one spike into several at no cost. Resend shows that a free open-source piece that is useful on its own can be the top of the funnel: React Email reached 10,000 stars and fed the core product ([Resend](https://resend.com/handbook/company/how-we-got-here)). Flying Money's equivalent is to make the verify middleware and the MCP server useful to someone who never sends a mainnet transaction.

Activation should be defined as one key event, as Supabase did with the first database created ([Craft Ventures](https://www.craftventures.com/articles/inside-supabase-breakout-growth)). For Flying Money the key event is **the first slip verified by a seller**, reached in under five minutes on testnet with a pre-funded demo key. Stars are a weak signal: HN score explains only about 8% of the variance in stars ([Daniel King](https://danfking.github.io/blog/2026/04/23/show-hn-by-the-numbers/)). Dev-tool benchmarks put visitor-to-signup at about 10% and 28-day retention at 23% ([boldstart](https://boldstart.vc/devtoolkit/an-alternative-to-nps-for-dev-tools/)). A realistic target for a good launch is therefore hundreds, not thousands, of developers who complete the quickstart.

### Sellers and API providers: the atomic network is one seller plus its own customers

Sellers are the hard side of the network, and Andrew Chen's advice is to build the smallest network that is useful on its own ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/atomic-network)). Flying Money's one-seller design is a feature here. A seller can offer a prepaid, capped tab to buyers it *already has*, the way eBay sellers brought PayPal its users ([Aakash Gupta](https://www.aakashg.com/paypal-the-original-product-growth-company/)). So the pitch is not "join our network". It is "give your existing API customers or regulars a prepaid budget: money up front, no chargebacks, verified locally in milliseconds, redeemed in one transaction." The integration must be pitched in lines of code, as Stripe did with "seven lines" and as MPP's launch sellers did with "a few lines" ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/blog/machine-payments-protocol)). Setup should be done by hand in the Collison-installation style ([Win With Flynn](https://winwithflynn.com/2026/04/13/collison-installation/)): the founders open the seller's repo with them and ship the middleware together.

The best-fit segments are MCP and API authors who already have a few paying users and dislike subscriptions and chargebacks, plus data feeds sold as "N calls a month". MPP's launch list (Browserbase, Parallel Web Systems) shows that browser, search and scraping APIs are where agents actually pay today ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/blog/machine-payments-protocol)). Apify's move to pay-per-event pricing and MCP hosting points the same way ([Apify docs](https://docs.apify.com/actors/publishing/monetize)). Publishers are a poor fit: Cloudflare already serves them, and crawlers need to pay many sellers ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/01/cloudflares-new-policy-pushes-ai-companies-to-pay-for-publishers-content/)). With no fee, Flying Money cannot offer Apify-style revenue share. It should compete on cost and ownership instead ("you keep 100%, and there is no platform between you and your buyer") and build a public seller list that shows verifiable redemption history. RapidAPI is the warning here. It peaked at about 40,000 APIs and a $1B valuation, then laid off 82% of staff and sold its technology unit ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/13/nokia-acquires-rapid-the-api-company-once-valued-at-1b/)). Listing counts are not demand.

For the shop and till mode, the lessons from physical QR payments are specific. Merchants adopt when payment is cheap, final and **unmistakable at the counter**. India's soundbox, which announces the received amount aloud, ended disputes over whether a payment had gone through ([Paytm](https://paytm.com/blog/payments/merchants-trust-and-support-paytms-new-and-improved-soundboxes-praise-nfc-sound-quality-quick-settlement/)). Programmes that depend on evangelism fade: 161 of 181 registered bitcoin businesses in El Salvador were non-operational by April 2025 ([Cryptonomist](https://en.cryptonomist.ch/2026/08/26/bitcoin-payments-el-salvador-decline/)). Flying Money's till therefore needs its own soundbox moment, a large confirmation with sound and the amount that a barista trusts without checking anything else. It should also be pitched as **a prepaid tab for regulars**, which gives the café cash up front and customers who come back, rather than as a way to accept crypto from strangers. That framing also fits the project's exclusion of offline cash between strangers. Count redemptions per merchant, never merchants onboarded.

### People: sell the outcome, keep the rail invisible, and wait for mainnet

The consumer message writes itself from the research, but its timing is constrained. People have not heard of the rail: **56% of Americans have never heard of stablecoins**, and intent to use them rises from 36% to 45% only when an existing financial provider offers them ([Visa Money Travels](https://usa.visa.com/about-visa/newsroom/press-releases.releaseId.22771.html)). Only 2% of US adults used crypto to buy something in 2025 ([KC Fed](https://www.kansascityfed.org/research/payments-system-research-briefings/us-consumers-use-of-cryptocurrency-for-payments/)). MoneyGram keeps even the word "stablecoin" out of customer copy ([Payments Dive](https://www.paymentsdive.com/news/moneygram-floats-stablecoins/808244/)). Consumer surfaces should talk about dollars, a tab, an allowance, "only works at this café", "ends Friday" and "what's left comes back to you". USDC, chains, gas and contracts belong in a "how it works" page for the curious.

The strongest consumer hooks are measured pains. Americans hold about **$27B in unused gift cards, averaging $244 per person**, and 34% have lost money to expiry, lost cards or stores that closed ([Bankrate](https://www.bankrate.com/credit-cards/news/gift-cards-survey/)), so "unlike a gift card, what's left comes back to you" is a clear line. Parents are the natural early segment: 48% of parents have used AI to shop, against 25% of non-parents ([NMI](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/nmi-research-consumers-want-ai-130000894.html)), and parents also buy allowances. Behavioural research supports labelled, prepaid, time-boxed pots as self-control devices: a planner commits in advance and a doer spends within the limit ([Skwara 2023](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/cb.2193); [Heath & Soll](https://bear.warrington.ufl.edu/brenner/mar7588/Papers/heath-soll-jcr1996.pdf)). The research also finds that rigid earmarks can backfire, so the end date and automatic return are part of the pitch, not fine print. The growth engine is two-sided in the GoHenry and Greenlight manner. Every budget sent is an invitation, and the holder's first coffee should end with "make one for someone else" ([Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/greenlight)). In emerging markets the dollar is itself the selling point: 47% of stablecoin users in a five-country survey cited access to dollars ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/316048/survey-visa-brevan-howard-castle-island-ventures-stablecoins-real-world-application)). "Send Mom a grocery budget at her corner store, in dollars" is a strong future story. But USDT dominates emerging-market retail and USDC is stronger in advanced economies ([Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/reports/2025-geo-crypto-report/)), and cash-out for small merchants is unsolved, so this is a later bet.

Consumer marketing for real money must wait. Testnet and unaudited software cannot responsibly be offered to people who do not read Solidity. Until then, the consumer narrative should run as a *demo* that shows the café tab and the allowance on testnet, clearly labelled, and serves as the most shareable story for developers and press.

### AI agents themselves: earn mentions, publish clean machine surfaces, respect directory rules

"Marketing to machines" has less evidence behind it than the industry claims. A July 2026 survey of 45 studies found **no technique with a stable, cross-platform causal effect** on organic discoverability. The gap between being recognised and being recommended is stark: ChatGPT recognises 99.4% of products when they are named but surfaces them in only **3.32%** of organic discovery queries ([arXiv 2607.14035](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14035v1)). The strongest correlate of AI visibility is off-site mentions. In Ahrefs' 75k-brand study, branded web mentions correlated at about 0.66 with AI visibility, against about 0.22 for backlinks ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-brand-correlation/)), and **YouTube mentions were the strongest single signal (~0.74)** ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260526119691/en/Across-75000-Brands-YouTube-Mentions-Are-the-Strongest-Signal-of-AI-Visibility-New-Ahrefs-Report-Reveals)). For a new brand, the lever is therefore to be named in other people's READMEs, comparison posts, forum answers and videos. On the team's own docs, the tactics with some evidence are explicit prices, dates and extractable numbers ([arXiv 2607.14035](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14035v1)), for example "0 protocol fees", "caps fixed at 100 / 1,000 USDC" and "verifies in N ms". Aim for specific long-tail questions such as "how do I cap what my AI agent can spend in USDC", not generic ones. Vercel's jump to 10% of signups from ChatGPT ([Rauch](https://x.com/rauchg/status/1910093634445422639)) came from owning a very common question, which Flying Money does not.

llms.txt, AGENTS.md and agent cards are hygiene, not discovery. Server logs showed **zero llms.txt fetches from GPTBot, ClaudeBot or PerplexityBot** ([Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/llms-txt-in-practice-adoption-evidence-2026)). Their real readers are coding agents integrating the SDK, which is still worth serving. Only 10 of 22,341 hosts probed had a fully conformant A2A agent card ([API Evangelist](https://apievangelist.com/2026/07/29/most-published-agent-cards-are-not-actually-a2a/)), so a conformant card on the demo seller is a cheap way to stand out. The official MCP Registry is the backbone that aggregators pull from ([MCP Registry](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/registry/about)). Glama grades licence, quality (whether tools introspect cleanly) and maintenance ([DEV](https://dev.to/kfuras/glama-clones-your-repo-smithery-proxies-your-http-mcpso-wants-a-markdown-line-18f5)), so the MCP server should list its tools without secrets in a read-only testnet mode. One hard constraint: Anthropic's Software Directory Policy **prohibits software that "transfers money, cryptocurrency, or other financial assets, or executes financial transactions on behalf of users"** ([Anthropic](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/13145358-anthropic-software-directory-policy)). A slip-signing MCP server is probably ineligible for the Claude Connectors Directory. The paths worth exploring are a read-only connector (check a budget, verify a slip) or the open registry, confirmed with Anthropic before any work is invested. The same policy bars tool descriptions written to make other software call them more often. That rules out "prompt SEO" in the MCP manifest, which is the right call anyway. Being the default an agent writes also means runtime retrieval: LLMs overuse already-popular libraries, in up to 48% of cases unnecessarily ([arXiv 2503.17181](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.17181v3)). Copy-pasteable typed examples and presence in framework example repositories matter more than naming tricks.

## Honesty and compliance are the brand, not a footnote

The most valuable thing Flying Money can say is exactly what it cannot promise. The FTC's Voyager settlement shows that "safe" and insurance-style claims about crypto funds bring personal liability for executives ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/10/ftc-reaches-settlement-crypto-company-voyager-digital-charges-former-executive-falsely-claiming)). The GENIUS Act, enacted and in force by **2027-01-18** at the latest, bars suggesting that a stablecoin is government-backed or legal tender ([Latham](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/the-genius-act-of-2025-stablecoin-legislation-adopted-in-the-us)), and the proposed FDIC rule says reserve deposits are not pass-through insured ([Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2026/04/implementing-the-genius-act)). The house vocabulary should therefore ban "safe", "secure", "guaranteed", "risk-free", "insured", "FDIC", "bank", "account" (in the deposit sense), "interest", "yield", "earn", "regulated", "approved" and, until an audit is done, "audited". "Escrow" should be avoided wherever it could imply a licensed escrow agent. The replacement phrases are factual and checkable: "a prepaid, capped budget held by a public smart contract"; "you can reclaim unspent USDC after the end date"; "USDC is issued by Circle, not by us"; "we never hold your funds or keys".

The risk statement should sit at the top of every surface, not in the footer: *Testnet. Unaudited. Do not use real funds.* The caps should be described as a limit on how much a bug can cost, not as a proof of safety: "On mainnet each certificate is capped at 100 USDC and each deployment at 1,000 USDC, in code nobody can change. The most a bug can cost you is what you locked." Engineering facts are fine as long as they are stated narrowly ("invariants I1–I7 are fuzz-tested at 256 runs × depth 50"), never as "formally verified" or "hack-proof". When an audit happens, name the auditor, the scope and the commit hash. Given the x402 wash-trading critique, which Visa and Artemis reportedly put at about 89% of dollar volume as wash, test or internal ([Bitget summary, secondary](https://www.bitget.com/news/detail/12560605260663)), the project should publish one public metric definition, *paid redemptions by buyers unrelated to the seller*, and label the demo seller as a demo everywhere.

How the project describes itself matters for regulation. FinCEN's 2019 guidance treats non-custodial software providers that lack "total independent control" over value as outside money transmission ([Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2019/06/fincen-consolidates-guidance)), and the GENIUS Act exempts self-custody software ([Latham](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/the-genius-act-of-2025-stablecoin-legislation-adopted-in-the-us)). Copy should say "open-source software" and "a public contract you use from your own wallet", never "we process payments", "our network", "your balance with us" or "we'll refund you". One flag for counsel: the repo's new hosted inbox and request routes, and any hosted demo seller or relayer, move closer to *operating a service* than *publishing software*. They deserve a legal read before they are promoted outside testnet.

Channels are constrained as well. Google prohibits ads for DeFi trading protocols and restricts wallet ads to certified advertisers, but it **allows ads for "businesses accepting cryptocurrency payment" and crypto educational material** ([Google](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/14009787?hl=en)). Meta requires written permission and a licence for software that lets users monetise, swap or stake crypto ([Meta](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/cryptocurrency-products-and-services/)). X banned crypto from its paid-partnership (influencer) programme from March 2026 ([Cryptonomist](https://en.cryptonomist.ch/2026/03/02/x-crypto-ads-policy-shifts/)). Reddit routes crypto ads through sales reps with risk disclaimers ([Reddit](https://business.reddithelp.com/s/article/financial-cryptocurrency-products-and-services-policy)). Singapore bars regulated DPT providers from advertising to the general public, including on social media ([MAS PS-G02](https://www.mas.gov.sg/regulation/guidelines/ps-g02-guidelines-on-provision-of-digital-payment-token-services-to-the-public)). The Philippine SEC is actively naming and blocking unregistered crypto platforms ([startup.ph](https://www.startup.ph/the-philippines-is-actually-enforcing-its-crypto-exchange-rules-now-and-bybit-just-found-out/)), and EU guidance treats almost any marketing that reaches EU clients as solicitation ([Maples](https://maples.com/regulatory-round-up/esma-reverse-solicitation-guidance-mica)). The practical rule for a zero-budget team is to stay organic and developer-facing: docs, GitHub, HN, technical posts and demos. There should be no paid consumer ads, no paid influencers, no geo-targeting of Singapore, and no "free money" giveaways. If starter budgets are ever funded for early buyers, they should stay within the cap and be framed as test credit for developers. None of this is legal advice, and a jurisdiction-specific review should precede any mainnet consumer marketing.

One project rule applies to all of this. **No hackathon or event name may appear on any public surface**: site, README, docs, blog, launch posts, MCP listings or social bios. Event-related material stays in `docs/submissions/`. Sponsor prize tracks for agent payments are still a useful channel. Builders there already use x402 and agent wallets, and a Flying Money starter that works alongside those tools can be picked up by hackers. The public-facing starter repo and any posts about it should describe the technology, not the event.

## A zero-budget plan in four gated phases

The plan below assumes two or three people, no paid media and the project's rules: no pushes, tags or releases without the founder's explicit request, and no mainnet transaction without human approval. It is ordered by leverage per hour. Each phase has a gate, and consumer-facing work is deliberately last.

Stars, listings and merchant sign-ups are vanity metrics that this category has already inflated.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "how do we get noticed among agent-payment rails" to "how do we become the budget primitive the rails lack". Big players are converging on caps, but as revocable settings layered on accounts and cards. Flying Money's lasting advantage is that its limit is money set aside in advance, under code nobody can change, so the worst case can be stated in one sentence. Its biggest vulnerabilities are single-seller fragmentation, USDC onboarding, being unaudited, and a near-twin in MPP sessions. None of them can be marketed away; they can only be disclosed first. A small team that discloses them first, reports only real redemptions and refuses safety language will be unusual in a category known for wash volume and overclaiming. In this market, that honesty is itself a competitive advantage.

The less obvious implication is that the product's constraints are what market it. The 100-USDC ceiling matches what consumers already say they would delegate. The one-seller rule makes each seller its own atomic network. The no-custody design gives the project its strongest regulatory self-description. And refusing to write MCP tool descriptions that steer agents is both what directory policy requires and the only kind of machine marketing with lasting value. The open unknowns are MPP's escrow terms, whether any read-only connector is eligible for the Claude directory, the final GENIUS rules, and whether stated consumer demand for caps turns into actual delegation. The team should resolve these before scaling any claim that depends on them.
